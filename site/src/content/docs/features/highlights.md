---
title: Highlights
description: How highlights get into Crossbill, and how you browse, filter, label and bookmark them.
---

A **highlight** is a passage you marked on your e-reader, imported into
Crossbill. It carries the passage and where it sits in the book — nothing you
wrote. Your own words about it live in a [note](../notes/).

## Getting them in

The [KOReader plugin](../../getting-started/koreader-plugin/) uploads highlights
from your device. Syncing the same book again is safe: Crossbill deduplicates,
so a highlight you already have is not added twice.

## Browsing a book

Open a book and go to its **Highlights** tab. Highlights are grouped by chapter,
in reading order, with a count next to each chapter in the chapter navigation.
You can:

- **Search** the book's highlights by their text.
- **Sort** the list newest-first or oldest-first.
- **Filter** by one tag and one highlight label at a time. Click a chip again to
  clear it.
- On a phone, reach the same chapters, tags and bookmarks through the filter
  button at the bottom of the screen.

Click a highlight to open it. From there you can step to the previous or next
highlight, copy its text, copy a link to it, add tags, delete it, and switch
between its linked notes and its flashcards.

## Highlight styles and labels

A **highlight style** is the highlighter appearance you used on the device — a
colour, a drawing style, or both. A **label** is the meaning you assigned to
that style: what you meant when you reached for the yellow highlighter.

Click the coloured dot on a highlight to name that style and pick the colour it
displays in. A highlight inherits at most one label, from the device — unlike
[tags](../tags-and-organization/), which you apply yourself and can apply many
of. Labels can be set per book or globally.

## Bookmarks

Bookmark a highlight from its dialog to save it into the book's index. Bookmarks
appear in their own list, ordered by page, so you can jump straight back to the
passages you keep returning to. A bookmark says nothing about reading progress —
it is just a way back.
