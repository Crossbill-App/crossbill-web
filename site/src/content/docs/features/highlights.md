---
title: Highlights
description: How highlights get into Crossbill, and how you browse, filter, label and bookmark them.
---

A **highlight** is a passage you marked in the [web reader](../web-reader/) or
on your e-reader. It stores the passage and its position in the book. To write
about a highlight, use a [note](../notes/).

## Adding highlights

- In the [web reader](../web-reader/#highlighting), select a passage and
  highlight it.
- On your e-reader, the [KOReader plugin](../../getting-started/koreader-plugin/)
  uploads your highlights. Syncing a book again does not create duplicates.

If you delete a highlight on the e-reader, Crossbill keeps it and marks it
**Deleted on the e-reader**. It no longer syncs to your devices. If you delete
it in Crossbill, it is removed from Crossbill and all devices.

If you highlight the same passage again on the e-reader, Crossbill restores the
old highlight with its tags. It also restores flashcards and bookmarks if you
deleted the highlight on the e-reader. Deleting a highlight in Crossbill deletes
its flashcards and bookmarks for good.

## Browsing a book's highlights

Open a book and go to its **Highlights** tab. Highlights are grouped by chapter
in reading order. The chapter list shows how many highlights each chapter has.

You can:

- **Search** the book's highlights by text.
- **Sort** them newest or oldest first.
- **Filter** by one tag and one highlight label at a time. Click a chip again to
  clear the filter.
- On a phone, use the filter button at the bottom of the screen to reach
  chapters, tags and bookmarks.

Click a highlight to open it. In the highlight dialog you can:

- go to the previous or next highlight
- copy its text or a link to it
- add tags
- delete it
- see its linked notes and flashcards

The search on this tab matches words. To search all your books by meaning, use
[semantic search](../semantic-search/) in the app bar.

## Highlight styles and labels

A **highlight style** is the colour and drawing style you used on the device.
A **label** is the name you give a style, such as "Key idea" for yellow.

Click the coloured dot on a highlight to name its style and choose the colour
Crossbill shows it in. A highlight has at most one label, which comes from its
style. You can set labels for one book or for all books.

Labels work differently from [tags](../tags-and-organization/). You add tags
yourself, and a highlight can have many of them.

## Bookmarks

You can bookmark a highlight from its dialog. Bookmarks have
their own list, ordered by page, so you can quickly return to passages you use
often. Bookmarks do not affect reading progress.
